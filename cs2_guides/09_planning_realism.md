# 09 — Realistic & creative city planning

## Start with a story
- two dollars twenty: root the city in history (why industry/waterfront exists) before painting grid. [S19]
- Infrastructurist: organic roads, mixed downtown, row houses, fishing harbor, industrial backbone, suburbs — build as a region of distinct districts. [S16]
- CPP realistic start: survey wind, water, aquifers, outside connections, then utilities, then grid. [S01][S33]

## Grid vs organic
- Downtown can be tighter grids; suburbs and farms get looser / bendier roads. [S21][S22]
- Seed arterials first ("catalyst" roads), then grow local fabric from them. [S19]
- FewCandy: new districts can intentionally be "non-gridy" vs CBD. [S21]

## Realism checklist (creators repeatedly do these)
- Road hierarchy + parking lots / street parking control (district policies / road upgrades — user may need to apply). [S18][S22][TRAFFIC]
- Service yards, rail depots, and utility plants treated as real land uses, not hidden. [S23]
- Surface parking "isn't nice" but a bit of it reads real. [S16]
- Pedestrian links and bike-aware block sizes when detailing (UrbanLoon mentions pedestrian-aware block sizing). [S20]
- Buffers: trees, distance, and zoning transitions between industry and homes. [S01]

## Creative techniques
- Theme districts (campus, marina, cemetery town, airport town) with matching signatures. [S23]
- Imperatur-scale vanilla showpieces prove you can go huge without mods — but Barney/Ephrata should stay human-scaled unless user wants megalopolis. [S34]
- Akruas: deep simulation / European PT patterns (S-Bahn style thinking) — useful inspiration for rail-focused regions. [S35]
- Detailing: props/mods may be unavailable or outside bridge control — describe what the user should place.
